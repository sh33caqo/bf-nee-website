Method

How BF-NEE links natural ecosystem loss to agricultural production and final demand

Overview

BF-NEE combines three data sets that each play a distinct role:

Data set Role
LandSHIFT Cropland extent per grid cell; crop composition per country
GlobES Composition of ecosystem classes (IUCN habitat scheme) and its change
GLORIA (MRIO) Share of each crop’s production that ends up in German final demand

The analysis covers the years 1995, 2000, 2005, 2010 and 2015 and therefore four five-year periods. Each period y-1 \rightarrow y is combined with the commodity and GLORIA factors of its end year y.

1 · Harmonising cropland

LandSHIFT is the authoritative source for cropland. In every grid cell the GlobES agricultural classes (14.1 arable land, 15.8 seasonally flooded agricultural land) are rescaled to the LandSHIFT cropland fraction L_{p,y}, and all other classes are rescaled so that the cell still sums to one. Six anthropogenic classes (14.4, 14.5, 15.4, 15.5, 15.6, 15.11) are removed beforehand.

\sum_{a \in A} G'_{p,a,y} = L_{p,y}, \qquad \sum_e G'_{p,e,y} = 1, \qquad A_{p,e,y} = G'_{p,e,y}\,A_p

1b · Intensive pasture

Since v3. Intensive pasture is part of the agricultural land.

LandSHIFT assigns a grazing fraction to every cell with livestock, both intensive pastures and extensive grazing on natural vegetation such as savanna. LandSHIFT separates the two only by stocking density, using the country mean as the threshold. BF-NEE follows this with one fixed threshold per country: the mean stocking density of the grazing land in 1995–2015, weighted by grazing area. A cell’s grazing fraction counts as intensive pasture L^{past}_{p,y} if its stocking density is above that threshold. Extensive grazing stays part of the natural GlobES cover.

GlobES is coupled to both agricultural land uses separately:

\sum_{a \in \{14.1,\,15.8\}} G'_{p,a,y} = L^{crop}_{p,y}, \qquad G'_{p,14.2,y} = L^{past}_{p,y}, \qquad G'_{p,e,y} = \tilde G_{p,e,y}\,\frac{1 - L^{crop}_{p,y} - L^{past}_{p,y}}{1 - \tilde G^{crop}_{p,y} - \tilde G^{past}_{p,y}}

Pastureland (14.2) is therefore an agricultural class and no longer counted as natural.

2 · Loss of natural ecosystems

For every natural ecosystem class e the gross loss between two observations is

\text{Loss}_{p,e,y} = \max\!\left(-\Delta A_{p,e,y},\,0\right), \qquad N_{p,y} = \sum_{e \in N} \text{Loss}_{p,e,y}

Expansion of one natural class does not offset the loss of another.

3 · Agricultural-expansion constraint

Not every ecosystem change is caused by agriculture. Only loss that coincides with a net increase of agricultural land (cropland + intensive pasture) in the same grid cell and period is attributed. Pasture converted to cropland is not counted as expansion:

\text{Gain}^{agri}_{p,y} = \max\!\left(\Delta A^{crop}_{p,y} + \Delta A^{past}_{p,y},\,0\right), \qquad R_{p,y} = \min\!\left(N_{p,y},\ \text{Gain}^{agri}_{p,y}\right)

R is split in every cell into a cropland and a pasture part in proportion to the positive changes of the two land uses:

s^{past}_{p,y} = \frac{\max(\Delta A^{past}_{p,y},0)}{\max(\Delta A^{crop}_{p,y},0) + \max(\Delta A^{past}_{p,y},0)}, \qquad R^{past}_{p,y} = s^{past}_{p,y} R_{p,y}, \quad R^{crop}_{p,y} = (1 - s^{past}_{p,y}) R_{p,y}

4 · Keeping the ecosystem classes

Since v2. Previously the constraint was applied to the total N_{p,y} only, so the information on which ecosystem was lost disappeared.

The constrained loss R_{p,y} is split onto the ecosystem classes in proportion to their original loss in the same cell:

R_{p,e,y} = \begin{cases} R_{p,y}\,\dfrac{\text{Loss}_{p,e,y}}{N_{p,y}} & N_{p,y} > 0\\[4pt] 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \sum_e R_{p,e,y} = R_{p,y}

Example: a cell loses 10 km² forest and 5 km² savanna, but cropland grows by only 6 km². Then 4 km² are attributed to forest and 2 km² to savanna. Footprints for forests, savannas, wetlands or tropical moist forests therefore add up exactly to the total footprint.

5 · Commodity shares from expansion

Since v2. Previously the static share of each crop in total cropland was used.

Ecosystems are converted by crops that expand, not by fields that already exist. For each country and period, the share of crop c is its area increase relative to the summed increase of all expanding crops:

S_{c,r,y} = \frac{\max\!\left(A_{c,r,y} - A_{c,r,y-1},\,0\right)} {\sum_{c'} \max\!\left(A_{c',r,y} - A_{c',r,y-1},\,0\right)}

Crops whose area shrank get S = 0; the shares of a period sum to one. If no crop expanded, all shares are zero and nothing is attributed.

6 · Attribution and indicator

The cropland part is attributed to crops with the commodity share and the GLORIA production share P_{c,r,y} (share of production in country r that is used for German final demand). The pasture part is attributed to Grazing livestock, whose P^{past} is the mean of the GLORIA sectors Raising of cattle and Raising of sheep and goats:

L_{p,e,c,y} = R^{crop}_{p,e,y}\,S_{c,r,y}\,P_{c,r,y}, \qquad L_{p,e,past,y} = R^{past}_{p,e,y}\,P^{past}_{r,y}, \qquad \text{BF-NEE}_y = \sum_p \sum_e \Big(\sum_c L_{p,e,c,y} + L_{p,e,past,y}\Big)

Summing L_{p,e,c,y} over any subset gives the footprint of that subset – e.g. of one ecosystem group, one country or one commodity. All values are areas in km².

Plantations (14.3), heavily degraded former forest (14.6) and several artificial aquatic classes are still not excluded and therefore count as “natural”. They are shown as a separate group so they can be judged – or excluded – explicitly.

Limitations

  • Commodity and GLORIA factors are national, not pixel-specific: the indicator does not identify the crop planted on a specific converted pixel.
  • The constraint is spatially conservative – loss is attributed only where cropland grows in the same cell and five-year period. Transitions that happen and reverse between snapshots are not captured.
  • Only intensive pasture is agricultural land; the split between intensive and extensive grazing depends on the country-mean threshold. Cattle and sheep/goats are weighted equally in P^{past} (no per-cell information on species).
  • The LandSHIFT–GLORIA concordance is not one-to-one (e.g. pulses and oil crops share one GLORIA sector).
